# Copywriting

## 1. Principles

- **Short.** Titles 1–4 words. Descriptions one line. Buttons a verb (+ object).
- **Benefit first.** What the person gets, then (optional) how.
- **Concrete.** Numbers, times, places: "14 min", "until 01:00", "−38 %".
- **Plain words.** No jargon, internal names, source acronyms (sources → credits).
- **Confident, friendly. Never cute in errors.**
- **No redundant labels.** A price needs no "Price:" in front. A title needs no "Title".
- **Capitalisation**: title case for buttons and menu items on Apple platforms ("Open in Maps"); sentence case for descriptions and footnotes. One rule per product.

## 3. Buttons and actions

- Primary: verb + result. "Directions · 14 min", "Add · $9.98", "Open the map".
- **Put the number that removes doubt on the button**: total price, time, count. No surprise after the tap.
- Never "OK" / "Submit" when a specific verb exists. Destructive: name the thing ("Delete Trip").
- Quiet shape, clear words. A shouting button (huge, saturated, all caps) reads as pressure.

## 4. Labels, badges, lists

- Badge: one fact, 1–2 words, normal letter spacing. No icon that repeats the word.
- Row: meaning in the first words. Detail right-aligned, secondary colour.
- Trust facts beside the object: "4.8 · 2,341 reviews" directly under the name.

## 5. Errors [useful, never blaming]

Say: what happened + what still works + what to do. Keep the last good data visible. Never raw codes alone; a code may follow the sentence for support. Never secrets, tokens, internal paths.

## 6. AI copy

- Answer first, reasons second, alternatives last.
- Show work in one line ("Checked 46 places, weather and air quality").
- 2–3 follow-ups as tappable chips. Ask before spending money ("Confirm table").

## 7. Marketing copy

- Hero: one promise, ≤ 8 words, one emphasised word. Sub-head ≤ 25 words.
- Feature title 2–4 words; body one sentence a person could say aloud.
- Proof over adjectives: real numbers, real sources, real product UI.
